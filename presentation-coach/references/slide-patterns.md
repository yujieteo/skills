# Coaching patterns by slide role

Use these to budget time and to decide what the notes should stress. Pick the
role from the slide's content, not its position.

| Role | Typical time | Emphasise | Point at | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title | 0:30 to 1:00 | The claim in the title, in your own words, and why the audience should care. | Nothing; look at the audience. | Reading the subtitle aloud. |
| Frame (stats, roadmap) | 1:00 to 1:30 | The stakes and the questions the deck will answer, so the audience can place each later slide. | Each tile as it appears (reveal steps). | Spending long on numbers that only set the scene. |
| Single-chart finding | 1:15 to 2:00 | The one comparison the headline makes; say it before you explain the axes. | The two marks or regions that carry the claim; name them. | Explaining every mark; introducing a second finding. |
| Dense chart (many marks) | 1:30 to 2:15 | How to read it (axes, colour, the reference line) in one sentence, then the outliers. | The labelled outliers, then the empty region that shows the contrast. | Letting the audience hunt for their favourite point. |
| Turn or twist | 1:30 to 2:00 | What changes about the earlier message. Pause before the reveal. | The mark that contradicts the earlier slide. | Sounding as if earlier slides were wrong; they were incomplete. |
| Decision or recommendation | 1:30 to 2:30 | What the audience should do differently on Monday; the caveat is part of the message. | The row or item you would act on first. | Presenting a rule as a forecast; hiding the sample-size caveat. |
| Sources and method | 0:30 to 1:00 | That the numbers can be checked, and how. | The endpoints or files named. | Reading every line; apologising for the data. |

## Timing

- Budget the opening and closing first (about 10% of the total together),
  then divide the rest by slide weight using the ranges above.
- Reveal steps cost time: count about 10 seconds per step plus what you say.
- Leave the last minute of the total unallocated for questions unless the
  user gives a strict slot.
- The **If short on time** list removes whole slides in order of least damage
  to the through-line; sources and second examples go first, the decision slide
  never does.

## Transitions

A transition names the question the next slide answers, in the audience's
terms: "That is where the points are cheap. So how much of it is luck?" Avoid
"next slide" and "moving on".

## Questions

Good candidate questions come from: the sample or time window, a definition
the deck uses without explaining, how a number was derived, a colour or
threshold choice, an obvious outlier, and what the data cannot show. Write the
answer using only the deck's own data unless you can verify more.
