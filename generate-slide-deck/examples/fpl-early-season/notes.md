# Speaker notes: Buy the defence, check the luck

## deck
**Takeaway:** The cheapest FPL points are in defence, but every hot start is partly luck, so check expected goals and the next five fixtures before you pay for it.
**Audience:** Assumed: informed FPL players who know the game, not statisticians. They know what a clean sheet is; they may not know what expected goals (xG) means, so define it once on slide 5.
**Total time:** 15:00
**Opening:** "Five gameweeks in, I want to show you where the cheap points are, and why I do not fully trust them."
**Closing:** "Buy the defence, but check the luck: expected goals and the calendar decide how much of it to pay for."
**Numbers to know:**
- 9.1 points per £m: Bogle, the best value in the data; Haaland is 2.5.
- Five of the top fifteen scorers are defenders, all between £4.6m and £6.1m.
- 3.5 to 4.4 goals: how far below expected the four value defenders' clubs have conceded (roughly 0.7 to 0.9 a game over five games).
- Groß: 7 goal involvements on 2.6 expected.
- 2.4 versus 3.4: the easiest and hardest average fixture difficulty over the next five gameweeks (Fulham versus Liverpool, Leeds, Palace and Brighton).
**If short on time:**
- Cut s08-ownership first; it is the weakest link to the decision.
- Then s06-attack-luck; s05 already carries the luck argument.
- Then s11-sources, and say the sources in one sentence at the end of s10.
- Never cut s05 or s10; they are the turn and the decision.

## s01-title
**Time:** 0:45
**Emphasise:** The title is a two-part instruction: buy the defence, and check the luck. Both halves matter, and the rest of the talk earns each one.
**Say:**
- Name the moment: after Gameweek 5 of 2026/27, the first real sample.
- Give the claim in your own words, not the subtitle's.
- Promise the shape: where points are cheap, how much is luck, what the calendar allows.
**Transition:** "First, the ground rules, so we agree what a good buy means."
**Likely questions:**
- Why only five gameweeks? It is what exists so far; the deck says repeatedly that gaps are prompts, not forecasts, and it is rebuilt from live data (see slide 11).
**Watch out:** Do not read the subtitle aloud; the audience has already read it.

## s02-frame
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** Everyone plays the same £100m for 15 players, so value per pound is the game. Five gameweeks is also a small sample, and you are saying so up front.
**Say:**
- Reveal the three tiles one by one: about 11 million managers, £100.0m with a limit of three players per club, and 195 players with enough minutes (at least 270) to judge.
- Then reveal the three questions, one at a time. They are the roadmap.
**Transition:** "Question one: where are the points cheap?"
**Likely questions:**
- Why 270 minutes? Three full matches; below that, points per pound is mostly noise. It is a choice, and it is on the source line.
**Watch out:** Do not linger on the tiles; they only set the scene.

## s03-top-scorers
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** Five of the fifteen top scorers are defenders, and none costs more than £6.1m. That is the surprise the rest of the talk tests.
**Point at:** The blue bars: Tarkowski, Bogle, De Cuyper, Gvardiol, Vušković. Then the price column on the right, next to Haaland at £15.6m.
**Say:**
- Bold names on the left are the defenders; read the five names.

- Note the leader is a midfielder, Groß, at £5.8m; this is not only a defenders story.
**Transition:** "Points are one thing; points per pound is the real test. Do defenders win that too?"
**Likely questions:**
- Is it just a top-15 cutoff effect? The last three places tie on 32 points and the next player has 31, so the cut is clean.
**Watch out:** This is total points after five gameweeks, not a forecast.

## s04-value
**Time:** 1:30
**Emphasise:** Defenders sit above the value line; Haaland sits far to the right and below it. Bogle returns 9.1 points per £m and Haaland 2.5.
**Point at:** The dashed line first ("6 points per £m"), then the blue dots above it in the top left, then Haaland alone on the right.
**Say:**
- Explain the axes in one sentence: price across, points up, so up and to the left is cheap points.
- The line is a guide at 6 points per £m, not a rule.
- Haaland is not a bad player here; he is an expensive one, owned by 73.8% of managers (slide 8 shows it).
**Transition:** "So the cheap points are in defence. How much of that is luck?"
**Likely questions:**
- Why is Haaland in the squad at all? Owning him is about matching the majority; the deck only claims he is poor value per pound, not that he is a bad pick.
**Watch out:** Do not turn 2.5 versus 9.1 into "sell Haaland"; the chart says value, not the decision.

## s05-defence-luck
**Time:** 2:00
**Emphasise:** This is the turn. The clubs behind the four best-value defenders have conceded 3.5 to 4.4 goals fewer than expected, so part of the value is borrowed. Pause before the numbers.
**Point at:** The four annotated rows on the left (Manchester City, Everton, Brighton, Leeds), then the whole right panel to show that the other clubs concede more than expected.
**Say:**
- Define expected goals against once: what the chances a team allowed should have cost it. Hollow dot is expected, filled dot is actual; blue means fewer conceded than expected.
- Name the defenders beside each club: Gvardiol, Tarkowski, De Cuyper and Vušković, Bogle.
- Translate the gap: roughly 0.7 to 0.9 goals a game over five games.
**Transition:** "Defences are not the only place luck shows up. What about attacks?"
**Likely questions:**
- Does this mean these defenders will collapse? No; it means expect more goals against than so far. The gap is about 0.7 to 0.9 a game, not a disaster.
- How is expected goals against measured? A regular starter's figure; goals against come from finished fixtures.
**Watch out:** Arsenal, top right, concede exactly what is expected (4.0): a good defence without luck. Say so, or the slide reads as "good defences are all lucky".

## s06-attack-luck
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** The same test on attacks: Brighton and Newcastle score well above their chances, and Coventry, Sunderland, Forest and Spurs well below.
**Point at:** The dashed "goals = expected" line, then Brighton at the top right and Coventry at the bottom left.
**Say:**
- Above the line is scoring more than chances warranted; below is less.
- Brighton have scored 16 from about 11.6 expected; Coventry one from 4.9.
- The four clubs below the line are where attackers might be bought before the goals arrive.
**Transition:** "The same idea works one player at a time."
**Likely questions:**
- Chelsea and Manchester City are red too; why not in the headline? The headline names the extremes; Chelsea are also the worst on goals against (12, slide 5), so they are volatile at both ends.
**Watch out:** Expected goals is an estimate built from a club's players, and five games is a small sample; call these clubs "candidates", not certainties.

## s07-player-gap
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** Groß has seven goal involvements on 2.6 expected; Thiago has one on 3.3. The gap, not the points, tells you who is a sell-high and who is a buy-low.
**Point at:** The top red bar (Groß, +4.4), then the bottom blue bar (Thiago, −2.3).
**Say:**
- Red bars are players scoring above what their chances suggest; blue bars below.
- Goal involvements are goals plus assists; xGI is the expected version of the same.
- Read the labels aloud for two players, using the "7 vs 2.6" form.
**Transition:** "So who is everyone buying? Is the crowd already ahead of this?"
**Likely questions:**
- Should I sell Groß? He is a candidate to sell high, but check his minutes, role and next fixtures before acting; the gap alone is not a forecast.
**Watch out:** Goalkeepers are excluded, and only players with at least 270 minutes are shown.

## s08-ownership
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** Ownership records past transfers, not present form. Szoboszlai is owned by about a third of managers on a form of 2.7, while Bogle, Schade and Mitchell are hot and lightly owned.
**Point at:** The red dots in the lower middle (Palmer, Cherki, Szoboszlai), then the blue dots in the top left.
**Say:**
- Define the colours from the legend and footnote: hot is form 7.5 or more with under 20% ownership; cold is form 4 or less with over 25%.
- Groß is the exception: high form and already owned by 29%.
- Haaland is the far right outlier.
**Transition:** "Knowing who is out of form is half of it. The other half is what the next games allow."
**Likely questions:**
- How is form defined? Average points over the last few gameweeks, straight from the FPL data.
**Watch out:** The thresholds (7.5, 4, 20%, 25%) are my choices to make the pattern legible, not FPL definitions.

## s09-fixtures
**Time:** 1:15
**Emphasise:** The calendar changes the answer: Fulham have the kindest next five fixtures at 2.4, while Liverpool, Leeds, Palace and Brighton average 3.4.
**Point at:** The Fulham row at the top left (all pale), then Brighton and Leeds in the right panel with the dark cells.
**Say:**
- Difficulty is FPL's own 2-to-5 rating, not ours.
- Capitals are home games, lowercase are away.
- Notice that Leeds and Brighton, the clubs of Bogle and De Cuyper on the next slide, have the hardest runs.
**Transition:** "Put all three checks together on the four defenders."
**Likely questions:**
- Who sets the difficulty? FPL does, per fixture; it is a rating, not our model.
**Watch out:** Averages over five games hide double gameweeks; the tooltip lists every fixture.

## s10-decision
**Time:** 1:45
**Emphasise:** All four value defenders come with a flag: their club's goals against are running at least 3.5 below expected, and Bogle's and De Cuyper's clubs also face the hardest fixtures. Great price, borrowed luck. Buy for the price, not the clean sheets already banked.
**Point at:** The red flags in the last two columns, then Gvardiol and Tarkowski, who each have only one flag.
**Say:**
- Walk one row, Bogle: 9.1 points per £m, but −3.5 on goals against and a 3.4 fixture run.
- A flag means goals against 3 or more below expected, or fixtures averaging 3.3 or harder.
- End on the callout: five games is small, so treat flags as prompts to check minutes and role.
**Transition:** "That is the argument; here is how to check it yourself."
**Likely questions:**
- So do I buy them or not? The deck says buy them for price and hold the luck loosely: none of the flags says avoid, and nothing here forecasts clean sheets.
- Why 3 and 3.3 for the flags? They are round thresholds chosen for this deck, and they are stated on the slide.
**Watch out:** Do not present the flags as a model; it is a checklist with two tests.
